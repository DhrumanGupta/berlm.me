This blog is meant as a supplementary reading to Songlin Yang's [DeltaNet Blog Series](https://sustcsonglin.github.io/blog/2024/deltanet-1/). I would recommend reading it first, otherwise this may feel incomplete. I repeat parts from her blog for the sake of completeness.

I then extend it to introduce Gated Delta Networks (GDN), Kimi Delta Attention (KDA), finally, Gated Delta Networks 2 (GDN2).

Notations: similar to Songlin's blog, I use CAPITAL BOLD letters to represent matrices, lowercase bold letters to represent vectors, and regular lowercase letters to represent scalars.

$L$ denotes the sequence length, $d$ denotes the embedding dimension. So, $\bold{Q}, \bold{K}, \bold{V} \in \R^{L \times d}$, and $\bold{q_i}, \bold{k_i}, \bold{v_i} \in \R^{d}$ are the $i$-th row of $\bold{Q}, \bold{K}, \bold{V}$ respectively.

# Linear Attention

Let's first compactly derive linear attention (taken from Songlin's blog). I give my interpretation of the limitations.

Recall, that the standard softmax attention (henceforth referred to as softmax attention) is defined as:

$$
\bold{O} = \text{softmax}(\bold{Q}\bold{K}\T \odot M)\bold{V} \in \R^{L \times d}\\
\bold{o_t} = \sum_{i=1}^{t} \frac{\exp(\bold{q_t\T k_i})}{\sum_{j=1}^{t} \exp(\bold{q_t\T k_j})}\bold{v_i} \in \R^{d}
$$

Linear attention [@katharopoulos2020transformers] simply removes the softmax operation[^1]. This gives us:

[^1]: In the original formulation, they project the keys and queries to a feature space.

$$
\bold{O} = (\bold{Q}\bold{K}\T \odot M)\bold{V} \in \R^{L \times d}\\
\bold{o_t} = \sum_{i=1}^{t} (\bold{q_t\T k_i})\bold{v_i} \in \R^{d}
$$

By symmetry of the dot product and associativity, we can rewrite $\bold{o_t}$ as:

$$
\bold{o_t} = (\sum_{i=1}^{t} \bold{k_i\T \bold{v_i}})\bold{q_t}
$$

This has a nice interpretation. Express the sum as a state matrix:

$$
\bold{S_t} = \sum_{i=1}^{t} \bold{k_i\T \bold{v_i}}
$$

Then, we have: $\bold{o_t} = \bold{S_t}\bold{q_t}$, and $\bold{S_t} = \bold{S_{t-1}} + \bold{k_t\T \bold{v_t}}$. So removing the softmax essentially changes our attention mechanism to a linear RNN.

## What makes this appealing

All we have done is remove the softmax. How does this help? As the name suggests:
| | Softmax attention | Linear attention |
| ----- | ----------------- | ---------------- |
| Time | $O(L^2d)$ | $O(Ld^2)$ |
| Space | $O(Ld)$ | $O(d^2)$ |

So there is a clear case for linear attention.

## Limitations

We first must think of $\bold{S_t}$ as a key-value store &mdash; given key $\bold{k_t}$, we want to retrieve the corresponding value $\bold{v_t}$. One can assume that the keys are unit length (QK-norm ensures this is approximately true).

Now, if we look at $\bold{S_t k_j}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bold{S_t k_j} &= \sum_{i=1}^{t} \bold{k_i\T k_j}\bold{v_i}\\
&= \bold{v_j} + \underbrace{\sum_{i=1, i\neq j}^{t} \bold{k_i\T k_j}\bold{v_i}}_{\text{error}}
\end{aligned}
$$

To get $0$ error, we need all keys to be orthogonal. Since we can have at most $d$ such vectors, this model's expressivness is inherently limited.

### The JL Lemma

We know that in high dimensions, there are exponentially many approximately orthogonal vectors. So should that not make the case for linear attention? Let's see.

By the JL Lemma, a $d$-dimensional space has roughly $\exp(O(\epsilon^2 d))$ vectors with inner products less than $\epsilon$. Suppose we want to bound the error term to have norm less than $\delta$. Let the value vectors have norm bounded by $V$.

$$
\norm{\sum_{i=1, i\neq j}^{L} \bold{k_i\T k_j}\bold{v_i}} \leq \sum_{i=1, i\neq j}^{L} \abs{\bold{k_i\T k_j}} \norm{\bold{v_i}} \leq \epsilon \sum_{i=1, i\neq j}^{L} \norm{\bold{v_i}} \leq \epsilon L V
$$

To get an error of at most $\delta$, this gives us $\epsilon = \frac{\delta}{LV}$. Note that we have $L$ key vectors. Substituting this into the JL lemma, we get that we can have at most

$$
L \approx \exp(O(\frac{\delta^2 d}{L^2 V^2}))
$$

vectors with inner products less than $\epsilon$. Simplifying this, we get $L \lesssim O(\delta \sqrt{d})$.

So in this worst-case setting (we've assumed that key errors cancel each other out), the sequence length cannot be more than $O(\sqrt{d})$. So not only is it not exponential, but it is sublineaar!
